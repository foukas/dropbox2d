## Problem Statement

The single trapdoor seesaw shipped on 2026-10-07 and plays well, and the user says doors already spawn often enough. The tracked follow-up (TODOS.md, "Split double-door trapdoor") was originally justified by spawn frequency, so that case is gone. The user's actual goal is **variety**: a second door style that lives alongside the single door, with each biome favoring one style so the door becomes part of that biome's identity.

## What Makes This Cool

- **A small hatch that reads as a real door.** Two flaps hinged at the hole's edges open downward and meet in the middle, like a double-leaf bascule bridge. End-hinged flaps clear almost the full hole width, so the hole can be about 1.25–1.6 units wide instead of the single door's 3.1.
- **A different skill from the single door.** Each flap rests against a joint limit at level, held shut by a counterweight on the far side of its hinge. That gives a **genuine weight threshold**, which the single door never had:
  - landing near a hinge (a short lever) holds;
  - landing near the seam (a long lever) swings the flap open.

  So you *aim for the seam*. On the single door, by contrast, a dead-center landing is the slowest to open.
- **Biome signature.** Each biome has its own mix of door styles. In a new band, the doors tell you where you are, the same way the sky palette does.
- **Still pure physics.** No motor and no scripted velocity, matching the single door and the user's rule: "Flinging the ball in a direction that is not down is impeding the goal."

## Constraints

- **Pure physics.**
  - **Each flap:** a dynamic body hinged at its hole edge with a revolute joint. Its upper limit is level (0°), so it can't swing up past closed, and its lower limit is θmax (opening downward). No motor.
  - **Self-closing:** a counterweight point mass on the far side of the hinge, under the strip. As with the single door's keel, it's applied through `Body.setMassData()` as the last mass call, so there's no extra fixture, nothing to collide with, and nothing to draw.
  - **Settling:** angular damping, the same as the single door.
- **Closing-moment excess.** Each flap's net closing moment at level is
  `E = M_c·c − m_f·(W/4)`,
  where M_c is the counterweight mass, c its distance behind the hinge, and m_f the flap mass.
  - **E > 0** is needed, so the flap rests shut against its limit.
  - **Static hold threshold:** a ball resting at distance x from the hinge opens the flap when m_ball·x > E, so `x* = E / m_ball`.
- **Tip-opens invariant. This is the one that stops wedging.**
  - **The problem:** if only one flap opens, the half-hole is narrower than the ball (W/2 < 0.8). A ball could slide down that open flap and wedge against the other, still-closed flap.
  - **The static rule:** a load at the tip of either flap must open it, so a ball reaching the seam forces the second flap open too. That means `E < m_ball·(W/2 − tipMargin)`, with **tipMargin = 0.1**.
  - **Why static isn't enough:** in the real wedge, the ball sits partly on the open flap and presses on the closed flap's tip corner with a mostly *horizontal* contact. Only part of its weight acts to open that flap. So the static rule is **necessary but not sufficient**.
  - **The sweep must prove the dynamic case** with dedicated one-flap-open entries (see Next Step 3) that assert zero wedges.
  - **Consequence:** holding can only happen in the band near each hinge, never at the seam.
- **Clearance (with thickness and the hinge at the hole edge).** With both flaps open to θ, the tip-to-tip gap is about
  `gap(W, θ) = W·(1 − cos θ) − t·sin θ`, with t = PLATFORM_THICKNESS = 0.35.
  - It must be at least ball diameter + margin, so 0.8 + 0.1 = 0.9.
  - The exact tip-corner geometry lives in a pure function, the same way `SeesawGeometry.clearanceAt()` works for the single door.
  - **Minimum W by joint limit:** θ = 75° → 1.67; 85° → 1.37; 90° → 1.25.
  - **θ_pass(W)** is the smallest angle that passes. A sweep cell is feasible only if θmax ≥ θ_pass(W) + 5°, the same rule as the single door. Infeasible cells are excluded from selection.
  - **Expected-fail controls:** W = 1.1 at 90°, and W = 1.4 with only one flap open, must never pass the ball.
- **Combo while held (user decision, 2026-10-07): resting on a flap resets the combo.**
  - Holding is a safe stop, not a free combo. A ball stalled on a closed flap for ≥ 0.5 s counts as a platform touch (one BallTouchedPlatform).
  - A quick landing that drops straight through keeps the combo.
  - The stall-on-flap detection lives in GameplayScreen, and ContactDispatcher still never fires on a flap contact itself.
- **Door style roll and fit.**
  - **Rolling:** a SEESAW-slot roll picks the style by the biome's `doubleDoorShare`, the **rolled** probability, before the retry loop. Fit depends on style.
  - **Fit:** each style uses its own fit check (single: minFiller + 3.1 + minLip; double: minFiller + W + minLip).
  - **Fallback:** if the rolled style still doesn't fit after the retry loop, the side falls back to **NORMAL** (no cross-style fallback). Configured shares are rolled probabilities; realized spawn fractions are measured, not asserted.
- **Total door rate stays steady (user decision, 2026-10-07).** A double door fits far more spans, so a high share would raise door frequency in that biome. Each biome's seesawChance is retuned so its total door spawn rate (single + double, per flanking side) stays within ±10% of today's measured rate. The rate is measured by a headless spawn-rate check that samples the real row-generation rolls (Next Step 5).
- **Always an extra way down.** The row's guaranteed gap and gap-reachability math are untouched, the same argument as for the single door and WEAK.
- **Combo.** Dropping through a double door keeps the combo, so the flaps fire no `BallTouchedPlatform`, just like the single door's plank. The wall filler and lip still reset it.
- **Rampage.** Flaps are never breakable. The filler and lip stay plain `"platform"` (rampage-breakable), matching the single door.
- **Layout.** The side is laid out as wall filler | hole (two flaps) | lip, with the hole placed randomly in the span's slack, matching the single door (eng review D10). A side needs minFiller + W + minLip ≈ 1.9–2.2 units, so double doors fit far more spans than the 3.7 a single door needs.

## Premises

1. Double doors are a **new door type added alongside** the single trapdoor, not a replacement. A per-biome share gives each biome a door signature.
2. Pure physics, the same rule as the single door: two flaps hinged at the lips, each with a counterweight beyond its hinge, and no motor. A joint limit at level means a closed double door genuinely holds a light or off-seam landing and opens for a landing near the seam.
3. The same safety and combo contract as the single door: always an extra way down, never wedges (proven by the drop sweep before any wiring), and dropping through keeps the combo.
4. A small hatch: the hole is sized from the clearance math (about 1.25–1.6 wide), not the single door's 3.1.

The user agreed to all four. The research ([double-leaf bascule bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bascule_bridge), [stabilizing the meeting point of double-leaf leaves](https://heavymovablestructures.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Support-and-Stabilization-of-Double-Leaf-Bascule-Bridges.pdf)) produced the joint-limit-plus-counterweight hold. The tip-opens invariant came out of working through what happens when only one flap opens.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Tune by feel (not chosen)
buildDoubleDoor, a biome share field and one focused guard, with constants tuned by playtest. Rejected because the threshold and wedge problems would show up in device playtests, which is exactly the cycle the single door's three sweeps avoided.

### Approach B: Prove it, then build (CHOSEN)
- A pure double-door geometry: clearance plus the hold-threshold lever math.
- The drop harness generalized to both door models.
- A double-door sweep picks the constants, and a focused guard locks them in.
- Then the biome share field and the wiring.

Effort: human ~2 days / CC ~3–4 h. Risk: low. Cost: generalizing SeesawDropTest touches the single door's guard too, so the refactor has to keep that guard bit-for-bit equivalent.

### Approach C: One-sided flap (not chosen)
A single end-hinged flap that opens one way only. Rejected as a different shape from the double-door look the user asked for.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B, user-selected, matching the recommendation.

## Open Questions

- **Exact constants:** picked by the double-door sweep against the acceptance thresholds below, not by hand. Starting grid:
  - W ∈ {1.4, 1.55, 1.7};
  - θmax ∈ {85°, 90°} (feasibility rule above);
  - counterweight mass M_c ∈ {0.15, 0.3, 0.45} and arm c ∈ {0.3, 0.5}, as **separate axes** (mass and inertia depend on each, not just the product);
  - counterweight vertical offset fixed at d = 0.2 below the hinge (under the strip);
  - damping ∈ {3, 5, 8}.

  Cells with E ≤ 0 (the flap won't close) or that violate the static tip-opens rule are skipped.
- **Hold band width:** how much of each flap near the hinge holds. x* is its knob. Feel is decided in playtest within the band the sweep proves; the selection rule targets x* ≈ 0.4 of the flap length.
- **Per-biome shares:** placeholders are NEON_DEPTHS 0.2 and FLOATING_VOID 0.8, meaning the fraction of SEESAW sides that are double doors. Tuned by feel.
- **Type modeling:** a new `PlatformType.DOUBLE_DOOR` rolled inside the SEESAW slot by the biome share, or a style flag on SEESAW. Settle at eng review.
- **Row storage:** a double-door side has two flaps, two fulcrums and two joints. Whether that means more PlatformRow fields or a per-side door record is an eng-review question.
- **Flap tag:** reuse `"seesawPlank"` (already outside the combo chain and the breakable list) or a new tag. Eng review.

## Success Criteria

- **Landing bands.** Measure a landing by s, its distance from the nearest hinge (s = W/2 − |x|, where x is measured from the hole center).
  - **Hinge band:** s ≤ 0.8·x*.
  - **Seam band:** s ≥ x* + 0.1.
  - **Transition:** everything in between.
- **No-steer landings in the seam band:** the door opens and the ball passes within 1.0 s of its final landing, without grazing the filler or lip (graze judged only when the ball's footprint is fully over the hole).
- **No-steer one-row landings in the hinge band: held is REQUIRED (user decision).** At least 80% of them must classify as held, across all layouts and dampings. None may wedge. Transition-band landings may hold or pass, never wedge.
- **Classification for two flaps.** A class is assigned at run end unless noted; "stalled" means speed < 0.2 m/s sustained for 0.5 s.
  - **passed:** the ball goes below the row while over the hole (recorded at any time).
  - **held:** stalled, the ball center over the hole, touching at least one flap, and every touched flap settled (|θ| < 5°).
  - **wedged:** stalled, not passed, and (over the hole OR touching a flap) with any touched flap unsettled (|θ| ≥ 5°). This covers flap–flap, flap–strip and stuck-on-an-open-flap cases.
  - **bounced out:** not over the hole and touching no flap.
  - **unclassified:** none of the above. This is a test failure.
- **Steered runs and roll-on entries:** pass, bounce out or hold, never wedge. Pinned-while-steering is decided by the 1.5 s release check, the same rule as the single door.
- **Tip-opens (dynamic).** The sweep must prove two things with dedicated entries:
  - a ball placed at rest on each flap's tip opens that flap;
  - **one-flap-open entries** never wedge. In these, flap A starts at θmax and the ball is released on A near its tip, or rolled down A into closed flap B. They run from rest and from 1-row height, with steering none/toward/against.
- **Combo while held:** a ball stalled on a closed flap for ≥ 0.5 s fires exactly one BallTouchedPlatform (a GameplayScreen-level test); a quick drop-through fires none.
- **Door rate:** each biome's total door rate per flanking side stays within ±10% of today's measured rate after retuning seesawChance.
- **Settling:** both flaps return to level (|θ| < 5°) within 1.5 s after the ball passes.
- **Coverage:** everything above holds for every Biome.values() ball damping, both layouts (minimum and typical span) and the three hole placements.
- **Regressions:** the single door's focused guard stays green after the harness generalization.
- **Combo:** dropping through a double door keeps the combo; touching the filler or lip resets it.
- **Biome signature:** each biome spawns its configured share of double vs. single doors, and the playtest confirms the style tells the biomes apart.
- **No regression** to gap reachability, the single door, moving or weak platforms, or rampage.

## Next Steps

1. **Pure geometry:** add double-door functions to `SeesawGeometry`:
   - `doubleClearanceAt(W, θ, t)`: the tip-to-tip gap with both flaps at θ;
   - `holdThreshold(M_c, c, m_f, W, m_ball)`: returns x*;
   - `tipOpens(...)`: the static tip-opens check;
   - `fitsDoubleDoorGeometry(span, W, minFiller, minLip)`.

   Unit-test the boundaries, including W = 1.25 at 90° and a configuration that violates tip-opens.
2. **Factory:** add `SeesawFactory.buildDoubleDoor(world, wallX, gapEdgeX, y, params, placement)`, reusing `staticSegment` for the filler and lip. For each flap:
   - a fixture-less fulcrum at the hole edge;
   - the flap body with its origin at the hinge;
   - a revolute joint limited to [0, θmax] in the opening direction (mirrored for the right flap);
   - the counterweight via `setMassData` (last mass call; I about the hinge = I_flap + M_c·c²);
   - angular damping.

   Flaps share the reserved no-collide group with the strips and with each other.
3. **Harness generalization:** refactor `SeesawDropTest` around a door-model abstraction: build bodies, list the planks, report the hole span and the max |angle| for settle and wedge.
   - The single door's guard must stay bit-for-bit equivalent.
   - Add the double-door thresholds above, including the hinge-band hold allowance and the tip-opens entries.
4. **Double-door sweep** (`@Tag("sweep")`, its own CSV, e.g. `build/reports/double-door-sweep.csv`), over the grid in Open Questions. **Selection rule:** among cells meeting every threshold:
   1. smallest W;
   2. x*/flapLength closest to 0.4;
   3. fastest settle.

   Pin the result as `GameplayScreen.DOUBLE_DOOR` and add a focused guard for the shipped constants, including the controls and the one-flap-open entries.
5. **Biome share + door-rate check:**
   - Add a per-biome `doubleDoorShare` in [0, 1], threaded through every biome constructor.
   - Add BiomeTest checks: every share is in range, and at least one biome leans each way.
   - Add a headless spawn-rate check that samples the real gap/type/style/fit rolls (exposed as package-private statics, the same D2 pattern) and reports the per-biome door rate.
   - Measure today's rate first. Then retune each biome's seesawChance so the total rate stays within ±10%, and assert that in a test.
6. **Wiring:**
   - roll the door style before the retry loop (fit depends on the style);
   - add a style-aware fit check;
   - add PlatformRow storage for the flaps, fulcrums, joints and lip, with nulling and recycling;
   - draw both flaps;
   - keep the flaps out of the combo chain and the breakable list (tag decision at eng review), and invert or add the ContactDispatcherTest coverage;
   - add the stall-on-flap combo reset (≥ 0.5 s stalled on a closed flap fires one BallTouchedPlatform) with its test.
7. **Playtest** on desktop, then the Galaxy A56: the seam opens, hinge landings hold, nothing wedges, flaps settle, the biome signatures read clearly, and nothing regresses.
8. **Wrap up:** update TODOS.md (resolve the double-door item, keep door shafts) and HANDOFF.md, and mark this doc SHIPPED.

### R2-3 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The expected-fail control 'W = 1.4 with only one flap open, must never pass the ball' contradicts the one-flap-open entries, which require that a ball rolled down open flap A into closed flap B never wedges. Since a stalled ball cannot be held on an unsettled flap under the classifier, the only acceptable outcome for those entries is that B opens and the ball passes. **Remedy**

> State that in this control flap B is locked closed (for example, joint limits fixed at 0 or a static body) so it only proves the half-hole cannot pass the ball, and state its expected class (not passed; wedged is expected here and does not count against the zero-wedge gate). Or remove it.
